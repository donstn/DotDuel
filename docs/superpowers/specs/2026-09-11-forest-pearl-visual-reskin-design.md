# Forest & Pearl visual reskin — Gemini-rendered art pilot

**Status:** design approved by user 2026-09-11, updated same day to a grouped-generation prompt structure, pending visual check after image generation.
**Branch:** `amazing-visuals-v2`.
**Supersedes:** the procedural-CSS ambient-layer plan in `visualization_improvement.md` (kept for reference/history only — this reskin uses real rendered art instead of animated SVG/CSS decoration).

## Goal

Replace the current procedurally-drawn look (CSS gradients, SVG shapes, glassmorphism) with genuine hand-painted-feeling art generated by Gemini, for one pilot theme — **Forest & Pearl**, the default theme — proving the whole pipeline before touching the other 7 themes.

## Decisions made during brainstorming (2026-09-11)

1. **Full scope**: dots, lines, board surface, background scenery, avatars, victory celebration, and UI chrome (panels/buttons) all become rendered art. Small icons (settings gear, back arrow, etc.) are explicitly deferred — not part of this pass.
2. **Art style**: glossy 3D mobile-game polish (Board Kings / Clash Royale / Candy Crush register) — rich lighting, smooth glassy/porcelain materials, premium and slightly stylized rather than photoreal.
3. **Rollout**: one theme (Forest & Pearl) fully painted and integrated before any other theme gets a prompt written.
4. **Piece concept**: dots are glowing glossy orbs; a completed line is a **string of light** connecting two dots — same "light" material family, so they read as one coherent object language.
5. **Line asset strategy**: a light-strand doesn't have a "wrong way up" the way a vine would, so one picture per *length* can be reused at any angle by rotating it in code. Every line on every one of the four board shapes (Triangle, Square, Rectangle, Rhombus) is between 1 and 9 dots long — so **9 lengths × 2 player colors = 18 images** covers every line that will ever be drawn, on any board, permanently. Confirmed against `src/geometry.ts`.
6. **Explicitly NOT painted this pass** (kept procedural, just recolored in CSS):
   - The board's outer decorative bezel/frame — already good hand-tuned work (Minkowski-offset frame, inner shadow); wrapping a picture around a perimeter that changes slightly per shape is a real hard problem, unlike a straight line, and not worth solving for v1.
   - Small UI icons.
7. **Generation method**: manual — user pastes prompts into the Gemini app/AI Studio themselves and hands back the resulting files. No batch API script.
8. **Board surface simplification (added on revision):** the four board shapes' felt textures don't need to be four separate paintings — the exact shape is already cut in code (a computed vector clip, not something the artwork has to match), so **one** richly detailed forest-floor texture is generated once and reused, clipped four different ways, for all four boards.
9. **Grouped generation (added on revision):** rather than one Gemini request per final picture, related pictures are requested together as a single "contact sheet" image with clear gaps between items, then sliced apart afterward in any basic photo editor. This cuts the number of times you have to run a prompt from 39 down to **11**, and — as a bonus — items generated together automatically share the same lighting and rendering quirks, which makes them look *more* consistent with each other than 39 independent generations would.
10. **No real transparency workaround (added on revision):** Gemini's image output doesn't give a true alpha-channel PNG even when asked for a "transparent background" — it paints something that just *looks* transparent, baked into flat pixels. The fix: every image that needs to be cut out (dots, lines, particles, avatars, buttons) is generated on a **flat, solid, glaringly unnatural background color** — bright magenta `#FF00FF` — chosen because it will never occur naturally in this theme's green/cream/jade palette. That color gets removed after the fact, either by hand with a basic editor's "magic wand"/color-select tool, or with a small local script (plain color-math, not another AI call, zero cost, one-time use) if useful once files are in hand.

## Total asset count

**10 Gemini generations → 36 final files** (down from an earlier 39-separate-generation draft, via the board-surface simplification and, after the first-pass review below, merging the two avatar-batch generations into one).

## First-pass review (2026-09-11)

All 10 (then 11) prompts were run once. Result: 7 of 11 were good on the first try and don't need rerunning (both line sheets, both backgrounds, particles, buttons, panel texture). Three had real problems:
- **Dots** ignored the requested magenta background and came out on white/light-gray instead — risky for cutting out the pale P2 orb cleanly.
- **Board surface** came back far too detailed/busy (large pearl clusters, big ferns) for something game pieces need to stay legible on top of.
- **Avatars**: the two-generation split broke the character "leveling" — level 1 came back as a stubby toy-mascot, levels 2-3 (separate generation) came back as full human-proportioned armored soldiers, an inconsistent family.

## Second-pass review (2026-09-11, same day)

Dots and board surface reruns both came back correct on the second try — **done, no further work needed on either.**

The avatar fix (merging into one 6-across generation) did not work — it failed differently: the background leaked into a real forest scene instead of flat magenta, the lineup came back duplicated into two rows instead of one, and robots 2-5 still weren't the same body shape as robot 1, the exact problem the merge was meant to fix. Two different one-shot text-prompt strategies have now both failed to hold a consistent body shape across the lineup.

**Decision: switch to sequential single-character generation inside one continuous Gemini chat**, where each message after the first explicitly instructs Gemini to copy the body shape from the image it just generated, rather than describing the shape in text alone. This is the standard technique for holding a character consistent across an image model and is a materially different mechanism than "write a more forceful paragraph" — it grounds each new generation in an actual reference image instead of hoping text redescribes the same shape twice. Trade-off: 6 messages instead of 1-2 for this category, accepted given two prior text-only attempts both failed on the same specific point and the user's stated priority is result quality over generation count.

## How the art will plug into the code (brief, for the later implementation plan)

- **Dots**: reused via `<image>` inside the existing board SVG, one asset per player, positioned/scaled exactly like the current procedurally-drawn circles are today. The "just placed" highlight and pending-claim state stay CSS glow/shadow effects layered on top of the same image — no extra art needed for those states.
- **Lines**: each of the 9 length images is placed as an `<image>` sized to the real pixel distance between the line's two endpoints and rotated to the line's real angle (both already computed today in `geometry.ts`/`Board.tsx`) — the art never needs to know which shape or direction it's being used for.
- **Board surface**: the single painted texture is used as a fill clipped to each shape's exact computed board outline (the outline itself stays vector/computed — only the *fill* is now a picture instead of a gradient).
- **Background scenery**: sits behind everything as a full-bleed CSS background image, portrait/landscape picked by viewport, same mount point the old ambient-layer plan proposed (a layer behind `#root`, not inside the board SVG).
- **Celebration particles**: swapped into the existing canvas-based `WinCelebration.tsx` particle system as textures instead of drawn rectangles — the physics/timing code is unchanged.
- **Avatars**: replace the current CSS-drawn robot art in `SidePanel.tsx` 1:1, same slots, same sizes.
- **Panel/button art**: used as a 9-slice-style stretchable background (a technique where the corners stay crisp and only a plain middle strip stretches) so one picture works at any panel/button size — this is a smart-stretch, not the "tiling" pattern we're avoiding; there is no visible repeat, just a flexible middle.

This integration plan will be fleshed out into a real step-by-step implementation plan (via the writing-plans skill) once the pilot art exists and you've eyeballed it — no point planning exact code changes against art that might still change during your visual check.

## Shared style guide (prepend to every prompt)

Keeping every image consistent depends entirely on reusing the same style language every time. Paste this block (or keep it visible) alongside each group prompt below — either prepended in the same message to Gemini, or as a saved "system" style note if the tool you're using supports one.

> **Style guide — Forest & Pearl, DotDuel:**
> Premium glossy 3D mobile-game asset render, in the visual register of high-end mobile puzzle games (Board Kings, Clash Royale, Candy Crush pieces). Smooth glassy / porcelain / liquid-light materials, rich specular highlights, soft rim lighting, subtle ambient occlusion, dramatic but soft studio-quality lighting, slightly stylized and rounded rather than photorealistic, clean and polished, no gritty or rough textures, no text, no numbers, no letters, no watermark, no logo, no signature anywhere in the image.
>
> **Color palette:** deep emerald forest green `#0d4a23` and warm glowing sage `#1c7a3d`, brightening to mint `#62cf90`, for Player 1 elements. Pale cream-pearl `#d3ecaa` and soft ivory glow `#f0fbcf`, brightening to pure white `#ffffff`, for Player 2 elements. Bright mint-green `#7bdb95` for magical light/accent effects. Deep jade-black `#15291e` fading to near-black `#02090b` for background depth and shadow. Mood: an enchanted, glowing forest clearing at dusk — moss, pearls, fireflies, soft magical luminescence.

Below, **[STYLE]** stands in for that whole block — don't retype it, just include it. **[KEY-BG]** stands for the cut-out background instruction, used on every group that needs to be sliced into transparent pieces afterward:

> Place every item on a single flat, solid, perfectly uniform background color of bright magenta `#FF00FF`, with no gradient, texture, or shading in the background itself — this exact color will be cropped/removed afterward, so it must not appear anywhere in the actual artwork.

## Open items for the visual check (after generation)

Per your instruction, nothing gets wired into the live game or judged as "done" until you've looked at the actual rendered images together. Things worth checking specifically at that point:
- Do the P1/P2 colors stay tellable apart from each other even if you squint / in grayscale (colorblind-safety) — this was a hard rule on the old procedural palette and should carry over.
- Do the 9 line lengths actually read as "the same family of object" getting longer, rather than looking like 9 unrelated pictures.
- Does the felt texture stay legible (not too busy) once real colored dots and light-strings sit on top of it.
- Do the magenta-keyed edges of each cropped piece look clean once the background color is removed, or is there a visible fringe/halo that needs a touch-up.
